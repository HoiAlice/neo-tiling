Given finitely many price vectors and outputs, we ask whether the outputs are generated by a neoclassical production model with a latent distribution of technologies. We prove a criterion in terms of reachable spectra, finite families of index sets defined by linear witnesses, and its closure, weak solvability, for prices on the boundary. The decision problem has a dichotomy: for two inputs it is a flow linear program and is solvable in polynomial time, while for three or more inputs it is NP-complete. Asking whether some prices in given boxes make the outputs weakly solvable reduces to one query of a box oracle on the $2T$ box corners, and the oracle is exact in every dimension by column generation with cover inequalities. The least relative $\ell_r$ price perturbation that makes the data weakly solvable is then a monotonic optimization problem, which we solve by combinatorial Benders cuts derived from the certificates of the oracle, with certified lower and upper bounds. Computations on EU KLEMS and BEA--BLS data illustrate the method. The structural results are verified in Lean~4 with Mathlib; the scope of the verification is stated in \cref{sec:lean}.
