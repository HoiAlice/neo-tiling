\section{Dimension three: NP-completeness}\label{sec:hardness}

\begin{theorem}[main]\label{thm:hard-main}
From a loopless graph $G$ with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges one constructs explicitly at most $100(n+1)(m+1)$ rational prices $P\subset\Rpp[3]$ and a rational vector $y\ge0$ such that the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $y$ is neoclassically solvable at $P$;
\item $y$ is weakly solvable at $P$;
\item $G$ is 3-colourable.
\end{enumerate}
\hfill\lean{neoSolvable_iff_colorable}, \lean{weakSolvable_iff_colorable}, \lean{numPrices_le}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
Deciding neoclassical solvability and deciding weak solvability at fixed rational prices in $\R^3$ are NP-complete under Karp reductions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
3-colouring is NP-complete, and the construction of \cref{thm:hard-main} is polynomial: the coordinates are explicit formulas of $O(\log(nm))$ bits. Membership in NP: a certificate is a decomposition $y=\sum_km_k\ind{S_k}$ into at most $T$ spectra (Carath\'eodory) with its coefficients; reachability of each $S_k$ is checked by $\abs{S_k}$ linear programs and the decomposition by a linear system.
\end{proof}

The proof has three parts. A gadget on the paraboloid turns solvability into membership in the stable set polytope of a graph $H$ drawn in the plane (\cref{sec:gadget}). The drawing condition is checked locally on a lattice (\cref{sec:disks}). The graph $H$ is a lattice drawing of $G\mathbin{\square}K_3$, and membership of the point $s$ in $\STAB(H)$ is equivalent to 3-colourability of $G$ (\cref{sec:layout,sec:coloring}).
