\section{Dimension three: NP-completeness}\label{sec:hardness}

\begin{theorem}[main]\label{thm:hard-main}
From a loopless graph $G$ with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges we construct explicitly at most $100(n+1)(m+1)$ rational prices $P\subset\Rpp[3]$ and a rational vector $y\ge0$ with entries in $\set{\frac13,\frac23,1}$ such that the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $y$ is neoclassically solvable at $P$;
\item $y$ is weakly solvable at $P$;
\item $G$ is 3-colorable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hard-np}
Deciding neoclassical solvability and deciding weak solvability at fixed rational prices in $\R^3$ are NP-complete under Karp reductions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
NP-hardness: 3-colorability is NP-complete \citep{GareyJohnsonStockmeyer1976}, \citep[Problem GT4]{GareyJohnson1979}, and by \cref{thm:hard-karp} the map $G\mapsto(P,y)$ of \cref{thm:hard-main} is a polynomial Karp reduction \citep[\S2.4]{GareyJohnson1979}. Membership in NP: by \cref{thm:solvability,thm:closure}, $y$ is solvable (weakly solvable) at $P$ if and only if $y=\sum_km_k\ind{S_k}$ with $m_k\ge0$ and $S_k$ reachable (weakly reachable). By Carath\'eodory's theorem for cones \citep[Cor.~17.1.2]{Rockafellar1970} at most $T$ spectra suffice, and they form the certificate. The reachability of each $S_k$ amounts to $\abs{S_k}$ linear programs, one per witness (normalized by $\ip\xi\e=1$; in the strict mode one maximizes the least slack), and the coefficients $m_k$ solve a linear feasibility problem. Linear programming is solvable in polynomial time \citep[Ch.~13]{Schrijver1986}.
\end{proof}

Duplicating a coordinate, $p^{d+1}_t:=p^d_t$ for all $t$, preserves the reachable and the weakly reachable families: a witness $\xi$ extends by $\xi^{d+1}:=0$, and conversely a witness $(\xi^1,\dots,\xi^{d+1})$ at the extended prices gives the witness $(\xi^1,\dots,\xi^{d-1},\xi^d+\xi^{d+1})$ at the original ones. By \cref{thm:solvability,thm:closure} the two problems are therefore NP-complete for every fixed $d\ge3$.

\begin{remark}[scope of the formalization]\label{rem:hard-scope}
\Cref{thm:hard-main}, \cref{thm:gadget}, the lemmas of Sections~\ref{sec:disks}--\ref{sec:coloring} and \cref{thm:hard-karp} are verified in Lean (\cref{sec:lean}). The cost in \cref{thm:hard-karp} is counted by a program. The counter is advanced only by unit-cost primitives on numbers; numerals, coercions, Boolean connectives, tuple building and graph lookups are free. Three facts are taken from outside the formalization. The first is the NP-completeness of 3-coloring. The second is the membership of both problems in NP, which rests on polynomial-time linear programming. The third is the passage from unit cost to bit cost, for which only the bit sizes of the output are proved formally. In the absence of formal complexity classes, the formal statement is the explicit reduction with polynomial bounds of \cref{thm:hard-karp}.
\end{remark}

A gadget on the paraboloid turns solvability into membership of a point $s$ in the stable set polytope of a graph $H$ with a lattice drawing, whose edges may cross (\cref{sec:gadget}); the drawing condition is checked on a lattice (\cref{sec:disks}). For a lattice drawing $H$ of $G\mathbin{\square}K_3$, $s\in\STAB(H)$ if and only if $G$ is 3-colorable (\cref{sec:layout,sec:coloring}).

\subsection{The paraboloid gadget}\label{sec:gadget}

The strict and the weak mode, the relation $\gs$ and the families $\Spm(P)$ are those of \cref{def:modes}, here with $d=3$.

Let $\e=(1,1,1)$. For $a,c\in\R^2$ put
\[
\ell(a):=(a_1,\ a_2,\ -a_1-a_2)+(1+\abs a^2)\,\e,\qquad
\xi_c:=(1-4c_1+2c_2,\ 1-4c_2+2c_1,\ 1+2c_1+2c_2).
\]
Since $\ip{\xi_c}\e=3$ and $\ip{\xi_c}{(a_1,a_2,-a_1-a_2)}=-6\ip ac$, for $m\in\R^2$ and $\eta\in\R$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard-wit}
\ip{\xi_c}{\ell(a)-\ell(m)-\eta\e}=3\bigl(\abs{a-c}^2-\eta-\abs{m-c}^2\bigr),
\end{equation}
and $\xi_c\ge0$, $\xi_c\ne0$ if $\abs{c_1}+\abs{c_2}\le\frac14$. So $\xi_c$ is a witness for the point $\ell(m)+\eta\e$ against $\ell(a)$ exactly when $a$ lies outside the disk with center $c$ and squared radius $\abs{m-c}^2+\eta$. Moreover, for $a,b\in\R^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard-chord}
\ell(a)+\ell(b)=2\ell\bigl(\tfrac{a+b}2\bigr)+\tfrac12\abs{a-b}^2\e.
\end{equation}

For a graph $H=(V,E)$ with edges $k=\set{u_k,v_k}$, a set $I\subset V$ is \emph{independent} if no edge has both ends in $I$, and the \emph{stable set polytope} $\STAB(H)\subset\R^V$ is the convex hull of the indicators of independent sets \citep[Ch.~9]{GrotschelLovaszSchrijver1988}.

\begin{definition}[gadget]\label{def:gadget}
A \emph{gadget} on $H$ consists of points $a_v\in\R^2$, $v\in V$, and heights $\eta_k\in\R$, $k\in E$. Put $m_k:=\frac12(a_{u_k}+a_{v_k})$. Its $T=1+\abs V+\abs E$ prices, indexed by $\{z\}\sqcup V\sqcup E$ in any order, are the \emph{marker}, the \emph{rewards} and the \emph{heavy points}
\[
p_z:=\tfrac14\e,\qquad p_v:=\ell(a_v),\qquad p_k:=\ell(m_k)+\eta_k\e.
\]
For $s\in\R^V$ the \emph{output vector} is $y_z=1$, $y_k=1$ for $k\in E$ and $y_v=1-s_v$ for $v\in V$. The gadget is \emph{valid} if
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item the points $a_v$ are pairwise distinct and $\abs{a_{v,1}}+\abs{a_{v,2}}\le\frac18$;
\item $\eta_k>\frac14\abs{a_{u_k}-a_{v_k}}^2$ for all $k\in E$;
\item for every edge $k$ and every independent set $I$ there is a center $c$ with $\abs{c_1}+\abs{c_2}\le\frac14$ and $\abs{a_w-c}^2>\abs{m_k-c}^2+\eta_k$ for all $w\in I$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

In (c) the disk may depend on $I$ and avoids only the rewards of $I$; this allows the edges of $H$ to cross.

\begin{theorem}[gadget]\label{thm:gadget}
Let a gadget be valid. Then in each mode $y\in\cone\set{\ind S\mid S\in\Spm(P)}$ if and only if $s\in\STAB(H)$. If $s\le\mathbf 1$, then $y$ is neoclassically solvable at $P$ if and only if $y$ is weakly solvable at $P$ if and only if $s\in\STAB(H)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By (a) the coordinates of $\ell(a_v)$, and of $\ell(m_k)$, are at least $\frac78$, and $\eta_k>0$ by (b). Hence all prices are positive and $p_t-p_z>0$ for $t\ne z$, so $\ip\xi{p_z-p_t}<0$ for every nonzero $\xi\ge0$. Therefore every $S\in\Spm(P)$ that contains some index contains the marker. Next, let $S\in\Spm(P)$ contain a heavy point $k$ with witness $\xi$. By \eqref{eq:hard-chord}, $\ip\xi{p_{u_k}-p_k}+\ip\xi{p_{v_k}-p_k}=(\frac12\abs{a_{u_k}-a_{v_k}}^2-2\eta_k)\ip\xi\e<0$ by (b), so $u_k$ and $v_k$ are not both outside $S$. Hence if $S$ contains all heavy points, the rewards outside $S$ form an independent set.

$(\Rightarrow)$ Let $y=\sum_Sm_S\ind S$ with $m_S\ge0$ and $m_S>0$ only for $S\in\Spm(P)$. Discarding the empty set, which contributes nothing, every $S$ of positive weight contains $z$, and $y_z=1$ gives $\sum_Sm_S=1$. Then $y_k=1$ forces every such $S$ to contain every heavy point, so $I_S:=V\setminus S$ is independent. With $\mu_I:=\sum_{S:\,I_S=I}m_S$ we get $s_v=1-y_v=\sum_I\mu_I[v\in I]$, a convex combination, so $s\in\STAB(H)$.

$(\Leftarrow)$ Let $s=\sum_I\mu_I\ind I$ be a convex combination of indicators of independent sets, and let $S_I$ be the set of all indices except the rewards $w\in I$. Then $y=\sum_I\mu_I\ind{S_I}$ coordinatewise. We show $S_I\in\Sp(P)\subseteq\wSp(P)$ by strict witnesses against every $w\in I$. For the marker take $\xi=\e$. For a reward $v\notin I$ take $\xi_{a_v}$: by \eqref{eq:hard-wit} with $m=c=a_v$, $\eta=0$, the slack is $3\abs{a_w-a_v}^2>0$ by (a). For a heavy point $k$ take $\xi_c$ with the center $c$ of (c) for $k$ and $I$; by \eqref{eq:hard-wit} the slack is positive. Finally, if $s\le\mathbf 1$, then $y\ge0$, and \cref{thm:solvability,thm:closure} turn the cone condition into neoclassical and weak solvability.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Empty disks on a lattice}\label{sec:disks}

Let the vertices of $H$ be placed at points $\pi_v\in\Z^2$. For an edge $k$ with ends $A=\pi_{u_k}$, $B=\pi_{v_k}$ and a vertex $w$ put $L_k:=\abs{B-A}^2$ and $\zeta_k(w):=2\pi_w-A-B\in\Z^2$. The vertex $w$ lies in the closed disk with diameter $AB$ if $\abs{\zeta_k(w)}^2\le L_k$, and on its circle if equality holds. Let $C_k$ be the set of vertices $w\notin\set{u_k,v_k}$ on the circle of $k$.

\begin{definition}[lattice drawing]\label{def:drawing}
A \emph{lattice drawing} of $H$ consists of pairwise distinct points $\pi_v\in\Z^2$ such that for every edge $k$: (a) $L_k\le16$; (b) $\abs{\zeta_k(w)}^2\ge L_k$ for all $w\in V$; (c) $\abs{C_k}\le1$, or $C_k=\set{u_{k'},v_{k'}}$ for some edge $k'$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[empty disks]\label{lem:disks}
Let $\pi$ be a lattice drawing of $H$, $0<\sigma\le\frac1{16}$ and $\sigma(\abs{\pi_{v,1}}+\abs{\pi_{v,2}})\le\frac1{16}$ for all $v$. Then $a_v:=\sigma\pi_v$, $\eta_k:=\sigma^2(\frac14L_k+\frac1{512})$ is a valid gadget.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conditions (a) and (b) of \cref{def:gadget} are immediate. For (c) fix $k$ and an independent $I$, and let $C\subset I$ be the vertices of $I$ on the circle of $k$, ends included. Since $I$ is independent and by (c) of \cref{def:drawing}, $C$ contains at most one end and at most one vertex of $C_k$, and no two of its vertices are antipodal (the antipode of an end is the other end). Let $v:=\sum_{w\in C}\zeta_k(w)\in\Z^2$. Then $\abs v^2\le4L_k\le64$, and for $w\in C$ the integer $\ip{\zeta_k(w)}v$ is positive: it is $\frac12\abs{\zeta_k(w)+\zeta_k(w')}^2>0$ if $C=\set{w,w'}$, and $L_k$ if $C=\set w$, where $L_k=0$ would make $k$ a loop at $w\in I$. The key inequality is, with $\delta:=\frac1{256}$,
\[
\tfrac14\abs{\zeta_k(w)}^2+\delta\ip{\zeta_k(w)}v>\tfrac14L_k+\tfrac1{512}\qquad\text{for all }w\in I.
\]
On $C$ it holds since $\delta>\frac1{512}$. Off $C$, (b) and integrality give $N:=\abs{\zeta_k(w)}^2\ge L_k+1$, and $\abs{\ip{\zeta_k(w)}v}\le8\sqrt N$; after multiplication by $512$ it suffices that $128N-16\sqrt N\ge128(L_k+1)-16\sqrt{17}>128L_k+1$, which holds as $L_k\le16$. Now take $c:=\sigma(\frac{A+B}2-\delta v)$. Then $\abs{a_w-c}^2-\abs{m_k-c}^2=\sigma^2(\frac14\abs{\zeta_k(w)}^2+\delta\ip{\zeta_k(w)}v)>\eta_k$ for $w\in I$, and $\abs{c_1}+\abs{c_2}\le\frac1{16}+\frac1{16}\cdot\frac{16}{256}<\frac14$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Drawing $G\mathbin{\square}K_3$}{Drawing G box K3}}\label{sec:layout}

Let $G$ have vertices $0,\dots,n-1$ and edges $\set{e_k,f_k}$, $k=0,\dots,m-1$, and put $\underline k:=\min(e_k,f_k)$, $\overline k:=\max(e_k,f_k)$, $N_k:=4(\overline k-\underline k)\ge4$. The colors are $i\in\set{0,1,2}$. Let $x_1:=3$, $x_0:=x_2:=1$, $X_{k,i}:=24k+8i+8+2[i=1]$ and $j_*(k,i):=12k+4i+4$. The graph $H$ is drawn at lattice points as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Row $(v,i)$}: nodes $r_{v,i,j}$ at $(x_i+2j,\,8v+2i)$, $0\le j\le12m$, joined consecutively.
\item \emph{Triangle of $v$}: the three edges between $r_{v,0,0}$, $r_{v,1,0}$, $r_{v,2,0}$.
\item \emph{Column $(k,i)$}: nodes $c_{k,i,j}$ at $(X_{k,i},\,8\underline k+2i+1+2j)$, $0\le j<N_k$, joined consecutively; it fills the odd heights strictly between the rows $(\underline k,i)$ and $(\overline k,i)$.
\item \emph{Attachments}: $c_{k,i,0}$ is joined to $r_{\underline k,i,j_*(k,i)}$, and $c_{k,i,N_k-1}$ to $r_{\overline k,i,j_*(k,i)}$; these row nodes have abscissa $X_{k,i}+1$.
\end{itemize}

Row nodes have $x$ odd and $y$ even, column nodes $x$ even, $y$ odd, $x\ge8$; the height $8v+2i$ determines $(v,i)$, and $X_{k,i}$ determines $(k,i)$ by its residue modulo $24$. Hence the drawing is injective, and a row crosses a column by two perpendicular edges with a common midpoint that is not a node; no crossing gadget is needed.

\begin{lemma}[lattice drawing]\label{lem:drawing}
The placement of the nodes of $H$ described above is a lattice drawing of $H$ (\cref{def:drawing}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Row and column edges have $L=4$, attachments $L=2$ and triangle edges $L\in\set{8,16}$. For a row or column edge with midpoint $M$, the point $M$ has $x+y$ even and carries no node, so $\abs{\zeta(w)}^2=4\abs{\pi_w-M}^2\ge4$, with equality only at the four lattice neighbors of $M$; the two that are not ends can carry only nodes of one column at heights differing by $2$ (for a row edge) or consecutive nodes of one row (for a column edge), so two such nodes are joined by an edge. For an attachment $\zeta(w)$ has odd coordinates, and the two non-end points with $\zeta(w)\in\set{\pm1}^2$ have $x+y$ even, so the circle carries only the ends. For the triangle of $v$ a finite inspection of the box $[-1,4]\times[-1,5]$ around $(0,8v)$ shows empty disks whose circles carry, besides the ends, only $r_{v,0,1}$, $r_{v,2,1}$ and $r_{v,1,0}$, since the row $(v,1)$ starts at $x=3$ and columns have $x\ge8$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The coloring lemma}\label{sec:coloring}

Each node of $H$ has an \emph{owner} in $V(G)$ and a \emph{color}. The row node $r_{v,i,j}$ has owner $v$, color $i$, and is a \emph{copy} for even $j$ and an \emph{anticopy} for odd $j$. The nodes $c_{k,i,0}$, $c_{k,i,1}$ have owner $\underline k$ and are an anticopy and a copy; the nodes $c_{k,i,j}$, $j\ge2$, have owner $\overline k$ and are copies for even $j$; all have color $i$. Put $s_p:=\frac13$ on copies and $s_p:=\frac23$ on anticopies. Every edge of $H$ except the triangle edges and the \emph{junctions} $c_{k,i,1}c_{k,i,2}$ joins a copy and an anticopy of one owner ($j_*$ is even and $N_k-1$ odd); on these edges $s$ sums to $1$. The junction joins copies of $\underline k$ and $\overline k$ of color $i$. The triangle nodes are the copies $r_{v,i,0}$, the \emph{corners}, and $s$ sums to $1$ over each triangle.

\begin{lemma}[coloring]\label{lem:coloring}
$s\in\STAB(H)$ if and only if $G$ is 3-colorable.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(\Leftarrow)$ Let $\chi$ be a proper coloring. For $\tau\in\Z/3$ let $I_\tau$ consist of the copies $p$ with $\mathrm{color}(p)=\chi(\mathrm{owner}(p))+\tau$ and the anticopies with the other two colors. A copy--anticopy edge of one owner has exactly one end in $I_\tau$, the corners of a triangle have distinct colors, and a junction of color $i$ has both ends in $I_\tau$ only if $\chi(\underline k)=\chi(\overline k)=i-\tau$. So $I_\tau$ is independent. A copy lies in exactly one and an anticopy in exactly two of the sets $I_\tau$, hence $s=\frac13\sum_\tau\ind{I_\tau}$.

$(\Rightarrow)$ Let $s=\sum_I\mu_I\ind I$ be a convex combination over independent sets and fix $I$ with $\mu_I>0$. A copy--anticopy edge or a triangle $A$ meets every independent set at most once and has $\sum_{p\in A}s_p=1=\sum_J\mu_J$, so it meets every $J$ of positive weight exactly once. Let $r_{v,\chi(v),0}$ be the corner of $v$ in $I$. Along a row membership in $I$ alternates, so a row node lies in $I$ if and only if (its color is $\chi$ of its owner if and only if it is a copy). The same holds along both parts of a column, since the attachment row nodes have even index. Hence $c_{k,i,1}\in I$ iff $i=\chi(\underline k)$ and $c_{k,i,2}\in I$ iff $i=\chi(\overline k)$; as the junction is not contained in $I$, $\chi(\underline k)\ne\chi(\overline k)$, and $\chi$ is proper.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of the main theorem}\label{sec:hard-proof}

Put $B:=24m+8n+8$ and $\sigma:=\frac1{16B}$, let $H$ and $s$ be as in \cref{sec:layout,sec:coloring}, and let $(P,y)$ be the prices and the output vector of the gadget $a_v=\sigma\pi_v$, $\eta_k=\sigma^2(\frac14L_k+\frac1{512})$ for $s$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:hard-main}.]
All nodes have $x,y\ge0$ and $x+y\le B$ (a row node has $x\le24m+3$, $y\le8n-4$), so $\sigma(\abs{\pi_{v,1}}+\abs{\pi_{v,2}})\le\sigma B=\frac1{16}$. By \cref{lem:drawing} the drawing is a lattice drawing, \cref{lem:disks} applies, and the gadget is valid. Since $s\le\mathbf 1$, \cref{thm:gadget,lem:coloring} give the equivalence of (i), (ii) and (iii). The rows have $3n(12m+1)$ nodes and $36nm$ edges, the columns at most $12nm$ nodes and $12nm$ edges since $N_k\le4n$, and there are $6m$ attachments and $3n$ triangle edges. Hence $T\le1+96nm+6n+6m\le100(n+1)(m+1)$. The data are polynomials with rational coefficients in integers and $\sigma$, so they are rational, and $y_v\in\set{\frac13,\frac23}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Polynomiality of the reduction}\label{sec:hard-poly}

The \emph{size} of $G$ is $n+m$. The \emph{bit size} of an instance $(P,y)$ with $T$ rational prices in $\Q^3$ and $T$ rational outputs is $T$ plus the sum of $\lfloor\log_2\abs{\mathrm{num}\,q}\rfloor+\lfloor\log_2\mathrm{den}\,q\rfloor$ over all its entries $q$, with $\lfloor\log_20\rfloor:=0$; it is within a constant factor of the size in the sense of \citet[\S2.1]{Schrijver1986}.

\begin{theorem}[the reduction is polynomial]\label{thm:hard-karp}
Let $B=24m+8n+8$. The instance of \cref{thm:hard-main}, with the prices numbered explicitly (the marker, the row nodes, the column nodes, the edges of $H$), is a computable rational instance. Every price entry has denominator at most $2^{17}B^2$ and numerator at most $2^{18}B^2$ in absolute value, and every output lies in $\set{1,\frac23,\frac13}$. In the unit-cost model each price with its output is computed from its index with at most $127$ arithmetic operations, in total at most $12700(n+1)(m+1)$. Hence $G\mapsto(P,y)$ is a Karp reduction from 3-colorability to neoclassical and to weak solvability in $\R^3$ whose cost and output bit size are both at most $C(n+m+1)^k$ with $C=2^{40}$ and $k=4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each entry is given by an explicit formula in the index: the node positions are affine in $(v,i,j)$ or $(k,i,j)$ with integer coordinates in $[0,B]$, and a price entry is a polynomial of degree at most $2$ in $\sigma=\frac1{16B}$ with coefficients in $\frac1{512}\Z$. So its product with $512(16B)^2=2^{17}B^2$ is an integer, and as the entry has absolute value at most $2$, the bounds on denominator and numerator follow; all numbers involved have $O(\log(n+m))$ bits. In the unit-cost model each arithmetic operation and comparison on numbers costs one. The counter is advanced only by unit-cost primitives on numbers; numerals, coercions, Boolean connectives, tuple building and graph lookups are free. We count the operations in each formula. A price costs at most $120$ operations, the most expensive being a heavy point (two reward points, their midpoint, the lift and the height). An output costs at most $6$ and the enumeration one per index, so $127$ per price. With $T\le100(n+1)(m+1)$ this gives $127T\le12700(n+1)(m+1)\le2^{40}(n+m+1)^4$. Using $\lfloor\log_2x\rfloor\le x$ and $B\le24(n+m+1)$, the output bit size is at most $T(1+3\cdot2^{19}B^2+6)\le2^{40}(n+m+1)^4$. Correctness is \cref{thm:hard-main}, which holds for every numbering of the prices. Two external facts complete the passage to bit cost: the intermediate numbers of the computation are subexpressions of the same formulas and therefore also have $O(\log(n+m))$ bits, and the unit-cost model with numbers of $O(\log(n+m))$ bits is polynomially equivalent to the bit model, the size of rational numbers being that of \citet[\S2.1]{Schrijver1986} up to a constant factor.
\end{proof}
