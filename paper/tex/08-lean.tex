\section{Formal verification}\label{sec:lean}

The structural results of \cref{sec:solvability,sec:weak,sec:planar,sec:hardness,sec:corners,sec:lp} are verified in Lean~4 with Mathlib, in the library \texttt{NeoTiling} distributed with the paper. The library contains no \texttt{sorry} and depends only on the standard axioms (propositional extensionality, choice, quotient soundness). Each statement of the paper that has a formal counterpart carries the name of the corresponding Lean declaration; the e-companion lists the correspondence and explains how to build the library.

\paragraph{What is verified.} The two solvability criteria (\cref{thm:solvability,thm:closure}); the chain characterization and the flow LP in the plane, together with the column problem and the Farkas certificate (\cref{thm:chain,thm:lp,cor:col,cor:farkas}); the reduction from 3-colouring, including the size and the bit length of the instance and its cost (\cref{thm:hard-main}); the box (corners) theorem and the cover criterion behind the exact oracle (\cref{thm:corners,lem:cover}); the reduction of the least $\ell_p$ perturbation to boxes, the certificate of infeasibility, closedness of the feasible set, and correctness and termination of the polyblock method (\cref{thm:reduction,thm:certificate,thm:algo}).

\paragraph{Cost models.} Mathlib has no complexity classes, so complexity is verified in explicit cost models. Programs are written in a monad that carries a counter, and the counter is advanced only by designated primitives. For the reduction of \cref{sec:hardness} a primitive is one arithmetic operation or comparison on a number of $O(\log(n+m))$ bits; for the polyblock method of \cref{sec:lp} the membership and master oracles enter as hypotheses and the number of iterations is bounded explicitly. Hence the formal statements bound counted operations and iterations, not running times on a machine model.

\paragraph{What is not verified.} NP-completeness of 3-colouring and membership of solvability in NP, which rests on the polynomial solvability of linear programming; the column-generation oracle and its certificates as implemented; the construction of a rational $Q$ from a certificate of feasibility; the refinements of the implemented $\ell_p$ algorithm beyond the formal model (\cref{sec:lp}). The correspondence between the Lean definitions and the definitions of the paper is part of the trusted base; the definitions are short and are listed in the e-companion.
