\section{Formal verification}\label{sec:lean}

The structural results of \cref{sec:solvability,sec:weak,sec:planar,sec:hardness,sec:corners,sec:lp} are verified in Lean~4 with Mathlib, in the library \texttt{NeoTiling} distributed with the paper. The library contains no \texttt{sorry} and depends only on the standard axioms (propositional extensionality, choice, quotient soundness). The e-companion lists, for every numbered statement, the Lean declarations that prove it, and explains how to build the library.

\paragraph{What is verified.} The two solvability criteria (\cref{thm:solvability,thm:closure}); the chain characterization and the flow LP in the plane, together with the family of paths, the column problem and the Farkas certificate (\cref{thm:chain,thm:family,thm:lp,cor:col,cor:farkas}); the paraboloid gadget, the coloring lemma and the reduction from 3-coloring, including the size and the bit length of the instance and its cost (\cref{thm:gadget,lem:coloring,thm:hard-main,thm:hard-karp}); the box (corners) theorem and the cover criterion behind the exact oracle (\cref{thm:corners,lem:cover}); the reduction of the least $\ell_r$ perturbation to boxes, one-signedness, the cover inequalities, the certificate of infeasibility, closedness of the feasible set, and correctness and termination of the polyblock method (\cref{thm:reduction,thm:onesigned,prop:cover,thm:certificate,prop:closed,thm:algo}).

\paragraph{Cost models.} Mathlib has no complexity classes, so complexity is verified in explicit cost models. Programs are written in a monad that carries a counter. The counter is advanced only by unit-cost primitives on numbers; numerals, coercions, Boolean connectives, tuple building and graph lookups are free. For the reduction of \cref{sec:hardness} a primitive is one arithmetic operation or comparison on a number of $O(\log(n+m))$ bits; for the polyblock method of \cref{sec:lp} the membership and master oracles enter as hypotheses and the number of iterations is bounded explicitly. Hence the formal statements bound counted operations and iterations, not running times on a machine model.

\paragraph{What is not verified.} The NP-completeness of 3-coloring is taken from the literature. Membership in NP (\cref{cor:hard-np}) rests on the polynomial solvability of linear programming and is not verified. The same holds for the polynomial running time of \cref{cor:poly} and the running-time claims of \cref{cor:col,cor:farkas}. The column-generation oracle and its certificates as implemented (\cref{prop:oracle}) and the construction of a rational $Q$ from a certificate of feasibility (\cref{prop:rational-Q}) are not verified; the latter rests on the rationality of optimal solutions of rational linear programs, and Mathlib has no theory of linear programming, nor are the refinements of the implemented $\ell_r$ algorithm beyond the formal model (\cref{sec:lp}). The correctness of the two solvers behind the oracles of \cref{sec:lp}, the exact box oracle and the MILP master, enters the formal theorem as hypotheses. The correspondence between the Lean definitions and the definitions of the paper is part of the trusted base; the definitions are short and are listed in the e-companion.
