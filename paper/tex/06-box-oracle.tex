\section{The box oracle}\label{sec:corners}

Throughout, $d\ge2$ (except in \cref{lem:cover}, where $d\ge1$) and $y\in\Rp[T]$. A family $W=(W_r)_{r\in R}$ of points of $\Rp[d]$ may be indexed by any finite set $R$; $\wSp(W)$ is the family of its weakly reachable spectra, defined as in \cref{def:weak} for finite families of points (in $\Rp[d]$, except in \cref{lem:cover}, where any points of $\R^d$ are allowed), and $w\in\cone\set{\ind S\mid S\in\wSp(W)}$ is regarded as a function $w\colon R\to\R_+$. For two families $L=(L_t)_{t\in[T]}$, $U=(U_t)_{t\in[T]}$ we write $W:=(L,U)$ for the family of $2T$ points indexed by two disjoint copies of $[T]$, and $w^-_t:=w(L_t)$, $w^+_t:=w(U_t)$ for the values of $w$ at the lower and the upper corner of $t$. Boxes $L_t\le q\le U_t$ of zero width are allowed.

Let $V=(v_r)_{r\in N}$ be a finite family, $S\subset N$ and $z\in\R^d$. The point $z$ is \emph{supported} by $V$ off $S$ if $\ip{\xi}{v_r-z}\ge0$ for all $r\notin S$ for some nonzero $\xi\in\Rp[d]$, and \emph{strictly supported} if moreover the inequalities are strict. Thus $S\in\wSp(V)$ if and only if $v_t$ is supported off $S$ for every $t\in S$. Since $\xi\ge0$, both properties pass from $z$ to every $z'\le z$. By the separation theorems \citep[Thms.~11.3, 11.4]{Rockafellar1970}, $z$ is supported if and only if $z\notin\interior K_S$, and strictly supported if and only if $z\notin K_S$, where $K_S:=\conv\set{v_r\mid r\notin S}+\Rp[d]$.

\begin{theorem}[corners]\label{thm:corners}
Let $L_t\in\Rp[d]$, $U_t\in\R^d$ with $L_t\le U_t$ for $t\in[T]$, and $W:=(L,U)$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item there is $Q=(q_t)_{t\in[T]}$ with $L_t\le q_t\le U_t$ for all $t$ such that $y$ is weakly solvable at $Q$;
\item there is $w\in\cone\set{\ind S\mid S\in\wSp(W)}$ with $w^-_t\ge y_t\ge w^+_t$ for all $t\in[T]$.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, if $z_t\colon[0,1]\to\Rp[d]$ are continuous paths with $z_t(0)=L_t$ and $z_t(1)=U_t$, then (B) implies that $y$ is weakly solvable at $(z_t(s_t))_{t\in[T]}$ for some $s_t\in[0,1]$. The diagonal $z_t(s)=L_t+s(U_t-L_t)$ is such a path and lies in the box.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(A)$\Rightarrow$(B). By \cref{thm:closure}, $y\in\cone\set{\ind S\mid S\in\wSp(Q)}$. Property (B) is preserved under sums and nonnegative multiples, so let $y=\ind S$ with $S\in\wSp(Q)$. Let $S'$ consist of the lower corners supported by $Q$ off $S$ and the upper corners strictly supported by $Q$ off $S$. Then $S'\in\wSp(W)$. Indeed, let $c\in S'$ be supported by $\xi$, and suppose $\ip{\xi}{c'-c}<0$ for a corner $c'\notin S'$. Then $\ip{\xi}{q_r-c'}>\ip{\xi}{q_r-c}\ge0$ for all $r\notin S$, so $c'$ is strictly supported, hence $c'\in S'$, a contradiction. If $t\in S$, then $q_t$ is supported off $S$, hence so is $L_t\le q_t$, and $L_t\in S'$. If $t\notin S$ and $U_t\in S'$ with functional $\xi$, then $0<\ip{\xi}{q_t-U_t}\le0$, which is impossible. Hence $w:=\ind{S'}$ satisfies (B).

(B)$\Rightarrow$(paths). Write $w=\sum_km_k\ind{S_k}$ with $m_k>0$, $S_k\in\wSp(W)$. Normalizing $\xi$ to the standard simplex $\Delta$, the supported points are the projection of a closed subset of $\Delta\times\R^d$ along the compact factor, hence closed \citep[Lemma~26.8]{Munkres2000}; the strictly supported points form an open set. Fix $t$. The supported weight $\overline h(s):=\sum_km_k\mathbf 1[z_t(s)\text{ supported by }W\text{ off }S_k]$ is therefore upper semicontinuous in $s$, the strictly supported weight $\underline h(s)$ is lower semicontinuous, and $\underline h\le\overline h$. A corner in $S_k$ is supported off $S_k$, and a corner strictly supported off $S_k$ lies in $S_k$; hence $\overline h(0)\ge w^-_t\ge y_t$ and $\underline h(1)\le w^+_t\le y_t$. Let $s_t$ be the maximum of the closed set $\set{s\mid\overline h(s)\ge y_t}\ni0$. If $\underline h(s_t)>y_t$, then $s_t<1$, and $\underline h>y_t$, hence $\overline h>y_t$, slightly to the right of $s_t$, contradicting maximality. So $\underline h(s_t)\le y_t\le\overline h(s_t)$. Put $q_t:=z_t(s_t)$ and let $A_k\subset B_k\subset[T]$ be the sets of $t$ with $q_t$ strictly supported, respectively supported, by $W$ off $S_k$. Then $\sum_km_k\ind{A_k}\le y\le\sum_km_k\ind{B_k}$. If $A_k\subset R\subset B_k$, then $R\in\wSp(Q)$ by the argument of the first part: a functional supporting $q_t$, $t\in R$, is a weak witness of $t$, since otherwise some $q_u$, $u\notin R$, would be strictly supported. Write $y_t=\underline y_t+\theta_t(\overline y_t-\underline y_t)$ with $\theta_t\in[0,1]$ ($\theta_t:=0$ if $\underline y_t=\overline y_t$), where $\underline y:=\sum_km_k\ind{A_k}$ and $\overline y:=\sum_km_k\ind{B_k}$. Then $y=\sum_km_k\bigl(\ind{A_k}+\theta\circ\ind{B_k\setminus A_k}\bigr)$, and by \cref{lem:cube} each summand lies in $\cone\set{\ind R\mid A_k\subset R\subset B_k}\subset\cone\set{\ind R\mid R\in\wSp(Q)}$. Since $Q\ge0$, $y$ is weakly solvable at $Q$ by \cref{thm:closure}. The diagonal is continuous and nonnegative because $L_t\ge0$ and $U_t-L_t\ge0$, which gives (B)$\Rightarrow$(A).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The box oracle.} Given a finite family $W$ indexed by $R$ and bounds $\mathrm{lo},\mathrm{hi}\in[-\infty,\infty]^R$, the \emph{box oracle} $\mathcal O(W,\mathrm{lo},\mathrm{hi})$ decides whether there is
\[
w\in\cone\set{\ind S\mid S\in\wSp(W)}\qquad\text{with}\qquad \mathrm{lo}\le w\le\mathrm{hi}.
\]
With $W=P$ and $\mathrm{lo}=\mathrm{hi}=y$ the answer is YES exactly when $y$ is weakly solvable at $P$ (\cref{thm:closure}). With $W=(L,U)$, $\mathrm{lo}=(y,0)$ and $\mathrm{hi}=(\infty,y)$ the answer is YES exactly when (B) of \cref{thm:corners} holds; this is the \emph{box query}. Thus weak solvability and the box query are \emph{one} call of the same oracle, on $2T$ points and with inequalities in place of equalities in the latter.

\begin{remark}[$d=2$]\label{rem:oracle-planar}
For $d=2$ the oracle is one linear program: the system of \cref{thm:lp} in the weak mode with a variable vector $w$ in place of $y$ and the bound constraints $\mathrm{lo}\le w\le\mathrm{hi}$, where repeated points and points with zero coordinates are allowed, since the chain construction of \cref{sec:planar} extends to them verbatim.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The exact oracle for every $d$}

\begin{lemma}[cover inequalities]\label{lem:cover}
Let $d\ge1$, $W=(W_r)_{r\in R}\subset\R^d$ and $S\subset R$. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $S\in\wSp(W)$;
\item for every $t\in S$ no convex combination of the points $W_s$, $s\notin S$, is strictly below $W_t$ in every coordinate;
\item for every $t\in S$ and every $A\subset R\setminus S$ with $\abs A\le d+1$ the hull of $\set{W_s\mid s\in A}$ contains no point strictly below $W_t$.
\end{enumerate}
A pair $(t,A)$ with $t\in R$, $A\subset R$, for which the hull of $\set{W_s\mid s\in A}$ has a point strictly below $W_t$ in every coordinate is a \emph{cover present in $W$}. It gives the \emph{cover inequality} $x_t\le\sum_{s\in A}x_s$. A set $S\subset R$ \emph{respects} a set $\mathcal C$ of covers if $S\cap A\ne\varnothing$ whenever $t\in S$, for each $(t,A)\in\mathcal C$. Thus $\wSp(W)$ consists exactly of the sets that respect all covers present in $W$, and their indicators are exactly the $0/1$ vectors $x$ satisfying all cover inequalities.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii). If $\xi$ is a weak witness of $t$ and $\sum_s\mu_sW_s<W_t$ for a convex combination $\mu$, then $0\le\ip{\xi}{\sum_s\mu_sW_s-W_t}<0$, since $\xi\ge0$ is nonzero. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i). Fix $t\in S$. If $S=R$, any nonzero $\xi\ge0$ is a witness. Otherwise let $M$ be the matrix with columns $W_s-W_t$, $s\notin S$. By (ii) there is no $\mu\ge0$ with $M\mu<0$, hence no $\mu>0$ with $M\mu<0$. By Gordan's alternative \citep[Ch.~2, Thm.~2.4.5]{Mangasarian1969} applied to the system $M\mu<0$, $-\mu<0$, there are $\xi,\eta\ge0$, not both zero, with $M^\top\xi=\eta$. Then $\xi\ne0$ and $\ip{\xi}{W_s-W_t}\ge0$ for $s\notin S$. (ii)$\Leftrightarrow$(iii). One direction is trivial. For the other, a point of $\conv\set{W_s\mid s\notin S}$ strictly below $W_t$ lies in the hull of at most $d+1$ of these points by Carath\'eodory's theorem \citep[Thm.~17.1]{Rockafellar1970}. The inequality $x_t\le\sum_{s\in A}x_s$ fails at $x=\ind S$ exactly when $t\in S$ and $A\subset R\setminus S$, so the last claim is (iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[cover inequalities and complete monotonicity]\label{rem:cover-bernstein}
By \cref{thm:closure} every weakly solvable $y$ satisfies the cover inequalities $y_t\le\sum_{s\in A}y_s$ for the covers $(t,A)$ present in $P$ (\cref{prop:cover} below); with $A=\set s$ this is dominance, $y_t\le y_s$ whenever $p_s<p_t$. These are inequalities with coefficients $0,\pm1$, the class of necessary conditions studied for a fixed cost function by \citet[Prop.~5.2]{AgaltsovMolchanovShananin2018}, and they are the finite counterpart of the alternating-sign derivative conditions of complete monotonicity in the complete-data criteria \citep{HenkinShananin1990,Agaltsov2015}: dominance is the first difference, a cover with $\abs A=k$ a $k$-th order one. \Cref{lem:cover} says that for the \emph{generators} of the cone this counterpart is exact, the weakly reachable spectra are precisely the $0/1$ solutions of the cover inequalities, and this is what makes the pricing problem of the oracle below a pure $0/1$ program. For the \emph{cone} it is not: validity of all $0/\pm1$ inequalities characterizes membership only when the cone is discretely convex, which fails for a fixed $h$ already at $d=2$, $T=6$ \citep{Molchanov2013a}, and for a free $h$ and $d\ge3$ no polynomial description exists at all (\cref{cor:extended}). The exact oracle resolves this by generating the generators, not the facets.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Column generation with exact geometry (not formalized).}
The oracle $\mathcal O(W,\mathrm{lo},\mathrm{hi})$ is standard column generation. The master problem is the linear program for $m\ge0$ with $\mathrm{lo}\le\sum_Sm_S\ind S\le\mathrm{hi}$ over the sets $S\in\wSp(W)$ found so far. Its duals give weights $g\in\R^R$, and the pricing problem $\max\set{\sum_{r\in S}g_r\mid S\in\wSp(W)}$ is a mixed-integer program in binary $x_r$ with the cover inequalities of \cref{lem:cover}. These have coefficients $0,\pm1$, so solver tolerances do not affect feasibility. They are added lazily to a set $\mathcal C$ of covers present in $W$, and each candidate $S$ is verified in rationals. For each $t\in S$ either a cover is found and checked by an exact linear program on at most $d+1$ points, or a rounded witness $\xi$ is verified by substitution, with an exact linear program over all points if rounding fails. Floating-point witness constraints would accept sets that are not weakly reachable at degeneracies. After convergence the master is solved in rationals. If it is feasible, the answer is YES. Otherwise its rational Farkas vector $\lambda$, scaled to integers, is priced once more over the sets respecting $\mathcal C$: an improving set has value at most $-1$, so the optimality tolerance cannot hide it; it is added and the step repeats. If there is none, the answer is NO.

\begin{proposition}[exact oracle]\label{prop:oracle}
The answer of the column generation is exact. A YES answer comes with sets $S_k\in\wSp(W)$ and rational masses $m_k$, verified exactly; for the box query the prices $Q$ with $L\le Q\le U$ are built from them as in the proof of \cref{thm:corners}, and $y=\sum_j\mu_j\ind{C_j}$ with $C_j\in\wSp(Q)$ can be checked. A NO answer comes with a rational $\lambda\in\Q^R$ and a finite set $\mathcal C$ of covers present in $W$ such that, with the convention $0\cdot\infty=0$ for infinite bounds,
\begin{enumerate}[label=(F\arabic*)]
\item $\sum_r\lambda_r^+\mathrm{hi}_r-\sum_r\lambda_r^-\mathrm{lo}_r<0$;
\item $\lambda(S):=\sum_{r\in S}\lambda_r\ge0$ for every $S\subset R$ that respects $\mathcal C$.
\end{enumerate}
Such $\lambda$ is given by Farkas' lemma for the master system \citep[Cor.~7.1d]{Schrijver1986}. Conversely, (F1) and (F2) exclude every $w\in\cone\set{\ind S\mid S\in\wSp(W)}$ with $\mathrm{lo}\le w\le\mathrm{hi}$: each $S\in\wSp(W)$ respects $\mathcal C$ by \cref{lem:cover}, so $\ip\lambda w\ge0$, while the bounds give $\ip\lambda w\le\sum_r\lambda_r^+\mathrm{hi}_r-\sum_r\lambda_r^-\mathrm{lo}_r<0$. Property (F2) rests only on the correct solution of an integer program with integer coefficients.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[rational solutions]\label{prop:rational-Q}
Let $L_t\in\Rp[d]$ and $U_t$ be rational with $L_t\le U_t$ for $t\in[T]$, and let an exact YES certificate of the box query (sets $S_k\in\wSp(L,U)$ and rational masses $m_k$) be given. Then there is a rational $Q$ with $L\le Q\le U$ at which $y$ is weakly solvable, and it is computed from the certificate in rational arithmetic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take the diagonal in the proof of \cref{thm:corners}; there $s_t$ is the maximum of $\set{s\in[0,1]\mid y_t\le\overline h(s)}$. Since $z_t$ is nondecreasing and supported points form a down-set, the set of $s$ at which $z_t(s)$ is supported off $S_k$ is empty, $[0,1]$, or $[0,s_{k,t}]$ with $s_{k,t}=\inf\set{s\in[0,1]\mid z_t(s)\in\interior K_{S_k}}$. The polyhedron $K_{S_k}$ is rational, so $s_{k,t}$ is rational. The step function $\overline h$ jumps only at the $s_{k,t}$, so $s_t\in\set{0,1}\cup\set{s_{k,t}}_k$, and $q_t=z_t(s_t)$ is rational.
\end{proof}
